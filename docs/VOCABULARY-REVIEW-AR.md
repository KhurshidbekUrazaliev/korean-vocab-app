# Arabic vocabulary review, Days 1–50

On November 28, 2026, all 500 entries were read in lesson order. The review checked the Arabic spelling and vowel marks, the reading of each word or phrase, and the English meaning in the lesson. It changed 15 entries across 11 lessons. Each of the 50 lessons is now marked `reviewed`.

## How the entries were checked

For each entry, the Arabic was read first, then compared with the transliteration and English. The Collins lists supplied the lesson context, especially for travel, shopping, food and everyday expressions. Some course words occur inside phrases in Collins or were added as basic vocabulary. Where a gloss seemed too broad, the Arabic form admitted more than one reading, or the script and transliteration disagreed, the review used the references below. [Collins Arabic, 3000 Words and Phrases](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Dictionary/CD%20Resources/Collins_Arabic_3000_words_and_phrases.pdf)

A second spelling-to-reading check compared the consonants, short and long vowels, doubled consonants, article assimilation and hamza in every entry. The course spells long vowels with two letters and uses ‘ for ع and ’ for a hamza within or at the end of a stem. At the start of a stem, the vowel stands alone, including after the article, as in *al-udhun* and *sayyaarat al-ujra*. The article is written separately in transliteration with a hyphen, including in *al-imtihaan* and *al-istiqbaal*. The latter two nouns begin with hamzat al-waṣl. [Library of Congress Arabic romanization table, sections on hamzah and the article](https://wwws.loc.gov/catdir/cpso/romanization/arabic.pdf)

Ordinary nouns use the course’s citation pronunciation. Masculine adjectival endings in ـِيّ are written *-ii* in that reading. The first noun in a construct phrase retains the *t* of tāʾ marbūṭa, as in *kurat al-qadam*. The indefinite ending printed in the Day 42 hotel phrase is pronounced *-tun*. Verbs retain the past-tense pronunciation of the Arabic third-person masculine singular form and take an English infinitive as their gloss.

## Notes on individual readings

- **Family and clothing.** Arabic distinguishes the father’s siblings in Day 4. In *ibn al-‘amm*, *ibn* specifies the son of the father’s brother. Day 28 teaches the singular الْحِذَاء. Learners encounter its plural الْأَحْذِيَة in “shoe shop” on Day 30. [Family terms in Al Jazeera Learning Arabic](https://learning.aljazeera.net/ar/node/934)
- **Email, film and the hotel room.** Day 34 has a ḍamma on ت in إِلِكْتُرُونِيّ, which supplies the short *u* before *ruu*. Day 41 keeps the long vowel in the printed فِيلْم. In Day 42, the explicit ending of غُرْفَةٌ gives *ghurfatun* before *li-shakhsayn*. Collins uses this connected reading on PDF page 89. [Riyadh Dictionary, فِيلْم](https://dictionary.ksaa.gov.sa/result/فيلم) · [Collins, page 89](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Dictionary/CD%20Resources/Collins_Arabic_3000_words_and_phrases.pdf#page=89)
- **Foods.** Both *faraawla* and *faraawila* are attested for strawberry. The Day 23 spelling and Collins agree on *faraawla*. The short consonant cluster in the Day 25 reading *ruubyaan* for prawn also follows Collins, on PDF page 57. [Birzeit University, strawberry](https://sina.birzeit.edu/qabas/lemma/2021587000) · [Collins, page 57](https://resources.collins.co.uk/Dictionary/CD%20Resources/Collins_Arabic_3000_words_and_phrases.pdf#page=57)
- **Emergencies and wildlife.** Day 39 uses إِسْعَاف for an ambulance, as in Collins, and حَرْق for a burn injury. Day 47 uses *gazelle* for غَزَال, the precise animal name in the biology and veterinary dictionaries. Its pronunciation *sulahfaa* for tortoise or turtle is also attested.
